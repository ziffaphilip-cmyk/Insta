## Topic and angle
Gym intimidation is mostly social, not physical. The heavy weight isn't what stops women; it's the walk
into the free-weights area, the feeling of being watched, and rules nobody explains. Instead of
"just be confident", the video names the feeling precisely and makes it smaller.

## Who it's for
Women who train on machines or cardio and keep avoiding the free weights. Also experienced lifters who
remember the feeling and will share it with a friend.

## Why it's worth posting
Most intimidation content is either "nobody's looking at you!" or a list of tips. Naming the walk itself
as the hard part is specific and recognisable, and it sets up T16 (the practical follow-up) three weeks later.

## Hook (first 1–3 seconds)
> Nobody's scared of the dumbbell. We're scared of walking over to get it.

Visual: start the shot already walking from the machines toward the dumbbell rack, camera at hip height.

## Your input needed
- The bracketed line in the script: what actually made the walk easier for you? If the suggestion fits
  your experience, keep it; if not, tell me what did and I'll rewrite that part.

## Script
```
Nobody's scared of the dumbbell.
We're scared of walking over to get it.

Think about what that walk involves.
You leave the machines, where everything has a little diagram on it,
and you walk into the part of the gym
where everyone looks like they know exactly what they're doing.
And you're pretty sure they're all about to watch you.

They're not, by the way.
Most people over there are looking at their phone,
their own form, or the clock.

But I don't think the answer is "just be confident."
The nerves make sense.
It's a new room with rules nobody writes down.

What helped me was making the walk boring.
[Same time of day. Same three exercises. The plan already in my notes app.]
So the only new thing was where I was standing.

The weights were never the hard part.
The walk was.
And it gets shorter every time you do it.
```

## B-roll and visuals
- Hook: POV walk from machines to the dumbbell rack
- "little diagram": close-up of a machine's instruction sticker
- "everyone looks like they know": wide shot of the free-weights area (no identifiable faces)
- "phone, form, clock": three quick cuts (someone's phone, a mirror, the gym clock)
- "making the walk boring": your notes app with the plan; picking up the same dumbbells
- Ending: you mid-set, relaxed; final shot echoes the opening walk, so it loops
